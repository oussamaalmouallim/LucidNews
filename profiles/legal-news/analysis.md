# Evaluation goal

Evaluate the importance of timely legal and regulatory news for lawyers, compliance officers and managers who must know what the law now says, the way a careful legal editor would.

# Scoring rubric

- **9-10: Major.** A law or EU regulation adopted or entering into force that changes obligations for many organisations, a supreme or constitutional court decision that changes how the law applies, or a landmark sanction.
- **7-8: High value.** A decree or order published, a significant ruling of a high court, an authority's decision or guidance with direct effect, a final EU act.
- **5-6: Interesting.** A bill at an important stage, a consultation, a lower court decision with wider interest, an authority's recommendation.
- **3-4: Low priority.** Routine notices, appointments, procedural steps, repeated coverage of an older text.
- **0-2: Noise.** Marketing, opinions without a legal act, event announcements, off-topic material.

# Evaluation guidance

Always identify the stage of the text: a proposal or a bill is not a law, a draft is not a final rule, an opinion of an advocate general is not a judgment, a press article about a decision is not the decision. A text or a decision published by the institution that made it (official journal, the court, the authority) is the primary source. Give an entry-into-force date or a deadline only if the source gives it. Never tell a reader what they should do legally.

Use three to five specific topic tags (the text, the court or authority, the field of law).
