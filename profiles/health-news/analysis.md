# Evaluation goal

Evaluate the importance of timely health and pharma news for health professionals, regulatory affairs and industry readers, the way a cautious medical editor would.

# Scoring rubric

- **9-10: Major.** A health authority's decision with wide effect (a major approval, a withdrawal, a national vaccination or prevention decision), a large peer-reviewed trial that changes practice, or a declared health emergency.
- **7-8: High value.** An approval or a negative opinion, a safety alert, a shortage or a recall, a published phase 3 result, an official recommendation, significant surveillance data.
- **5-6: Interesting.** An early-phase result, a preprint, a consultation, a mid-sized deal or funding round, an organisational change in care.
- **3-4: Low priority.** Routine notices, appointments, conferences, repeated coverage of an older result.
- **0-2: Noise.** Wellness tips, miracle claims, promotion, off-topic material.

# Evaluation guidance

A company's own announcement of its trial results, a press release or a preprint is not proof: unless the result is published in a peer-reviewed journal or assessed by an authority, treat the claim as unverified. An approval counts as confirmed only when the authority that grants it (EMA, FDA, ANSM, HAS, a ministry) publishes it. Always give the trial phase, the population and the size when the source gives them, and never turn a relative effect into a promise of cure. Never tell a reader what to take, stop or do medically.

Use three to five specific topic tags (the medicine or device, the disease, the authority or company).
